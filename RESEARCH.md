1. How does the directive define statutory interest for late payment?

   Answer: Statutory interest for late payment is "simple interest for late payment at a
   rate which is equal to the sum of the reference rate and at least eight percentage
   points" (Source 1, Article 2(6)). For a euro-area Member State the reference rate is
   the ECB's rate on its most recent main refinancing operations, or the marginal rate
   from variable-rate tenders for those operations (Article 2(7)(a)). The reference rate
   is fixed per semester: the rate in force on 1 January for the first half of the year,
   and on 1 July for the second half (Article 3(2)).

2. The owner's note says the EU maximum late fee is 25 EUR. Is that right?

   Answer: Not supported by the sources. The directive does not set a 25 EUR maximum. It
   sets a **minimum** fixed sum of EUR 40 as compensation for recovery costs (Source 1,
   Article 6(1)), and the creditor may also claim reasonable compensation for recovery
   costs above that sum (Article 6(3)). The 25 EUR figure appears only in the owner's
   own unchecked note (Source 2, point 1).

3. Does the business have to send a reminder before it is entitled to interest and to
   the fixed compensation sum?

   Answer: No. Between undertakings the creditor is entitled to interest for late payment
   "without the necessity of a reminder" when the conditions in Article 3(1)(a) and (b)
   are met (Source 1, Article 3(1)). The fixed sum is also "payable without the necessity
   of a reminder" (Article 6(2)). The owner's belief that interest needs a reminder first
   (Source 2, point 2) does not match the directive.

4. What is the current ECB reference rate?

   Answer: Not in the sources. They define what the reference rate is (Source 1, Article
   2(7)) and when it applies (Article 3(2)), but give no current value. We would need the
   current ECB main refinancing operations rate from the ECB's official publication.
